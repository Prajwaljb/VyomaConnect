Vyoma's privacy policy

20 November 2026

Vyoma has no accounts, no servers, no ads, no analytics and no trackers. What it handles goes straight between your phone and the computers you pair it with, and nowhere else.

This covers the Vyoma app for Android, from Google Play, F-Droid, IzzyOnDroid or GitHub, and Vyoma's apps for computers. Vyoma is free software: anyone can read its source code and check what is written here.

How your data travels

Your phone talks only to computers you have paired with it, over your own Wi-Fi, a USB cable or Bluetooth. Every connection is encrypted (TLS), and each side checks the other's key, which you confirmed when pairing. Nobody runs a server in between, so the developer never receives, sees or stores anything you do with Vyoma.

What the phone app handles, and why

Each of these happens only when you use the feature and give Android's permission for it, and each can be turned off again in Vyoma's settings.

  • Notifications: shown on your PC, where you can reply to or dismiss them.
  • Texts and contacts: read so you can see and answer your conversations on your PC, and sent from the phone when you write one there. Vyoma's PC app keeps them only in memory and shows them only after you confirm on the phone with your fingerprint or PIN, and locks them away again when its screen locks.
  • Calls and the call log: who is calling, shown on your PC, which can silence, answer or decline; calls you start from the PC's Messages page.
  • Clipboard: what you copy goes to your PC, and the PC's copies come to the phone. Copying in the background, if you allow it from your PC once over a USB cable, works by reading Android's system log for the moment something is copied; nothing from the log leaves the phone.
  • Files: files you send, and, with all-files access, your PC browsing the phone's storage when you open it there.
  • Camera: scanning the pairing code your PC shows, and the phone as your PC's webcam when you turn that on.
  • Microphone: the webcam's sound, and the phone's own sound while its screen is shown on your PC (Android counts capturing what's playing as recording).
  • Screen: shown in a window on your PC while you choose to, with Android's own prompt each time.
  • Accessibility service ("Vyoma PC control"): while the phone's screen is shown on your PC, it performs the taps, swipes and typing that window sends. To type it reads and changes only the text field you tapped; Ctrl+C there sends the text you selected to your PC. It reads nothing else and keeps nothing.
  • Phone state and battery: your PC shows the phone's battery and signal.
  • Bluetooth: a link to your PC when Wi-Fi isn't there, and your own automation rules that start when a Bluetooth device connects.

The only things that reach the internet

  • Album art: when your PC plays media, the phone may load its cover picture from the address the PC's player gives (for a YouTube video, YouTube's thumbnail), as any browser would. That site sees your internet address, as it does when you visit it.
  • Links you open: a link your PC sends opens in your browser only when you tap it.
  • Updates on computers: Vyoma's computer apps ask GitHub once a day whether a new release is out, and you can turn that off on This PC. The phone app has no update check; your app store updates it.

What is kept, and where

Settings, the PCs you paired and their keys, your automation rules and Vyoma's own log stay in the app's private storage on your phone, and on your computers in Vyoma's folder there. Uninstalling removes them. The log never leaves your device unless you share it yourself, for example with a bug report.

No data is sold, shared with anyone, or used for advertising.

Children

Vyoma isn't directed at children, and it collects nothing from anyone.

Changes and questions

If this policy changes, the new one appears here with a new date, and the change is in the project's history on GitHub. Ask anything by opening an issue.
